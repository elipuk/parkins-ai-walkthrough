# Arrival

Cromwell Road, looking up. Ornate Victorian railings and a black lamp post in the foreground; behind them, the pale terracotta towers of Alfred Waterhouse's museum catching the warm low light, sky a clean blue above.

Waterhouse opened the doors in 1881 and the building is still doing the thing it was designed to do. The terracotta wasn't decorative whimsy — London in the 1870s was a coal-burning city, the air was filthy, and Portland stone turned black within a generation. Terracotta you could wash. So Waterhouse wrapped his Romanesque palace in fired clay tiles, sculpted with monkeys and pterodactyls and ammonites, and the building has shrugged off a century and a half of soot, war, and weather while looking, on a Friday evening in late May, more or less exactly as he intended.

The pay-off after the SWR saga. *We're here.*

The evening was Charlotte's idea — she'd organised the tickets, picked the Lates slot, picked the night. Graham came along happily. Without her this isn't an outing; it's a Friday at home. Worth saying once, here at the gate, before the bones and the photographs swallow the rest of the article.
