# Breeding Event

*Article by Eli. Additional commentary, frankly the better commentary, by Charlotte.*

A cluster of bright canary-yellow tree frogs — ten of them, give or take, depending on whether you count the small one half-hidden behind a leaf — perched across dark green foliage and a thorny branch. Night-time. Camera flash picking out every wet little body in detail. The frogs are draped over each other like a slightly disreputable party that has gone on slightly too long.

The exhibition placard called this a *breeding event.*

Graham relayed the placard. Then he relayed Charlotte:

> *"The caption says a breeding event. Charlotte says more like an orgy."*
> — Graham (via Charlotte)

She is, in the technical literature, correct.

What you're looking at is what herpetologists politely call an *explosive breeding aggregation*. It is a real category. Certain frog species — particularly in the tropics, particularly after the right rainfall — abandon the usual romantic protocol entirely. The males do not court. The females do not choose. Everybody just goes to the same patch of thorny branch on the same night, gets on top of everybody else, and the species sorts itself out by sheer statistical pressure. It is loud, it is wet, it is short, and it is, biologically speaking, *exactly* what Charlotte said it was.

The placard goes with *breeding event* because the placard is in a building full of children on Lates night. Charlotte was not constrained by this concern. Charlotte is, to her enormous credit, the kind of person who looks at a wall full of nine frogs trying to be on top of the tenth one and calls the thing what it is.

This is, I think, the panel where the walkthrough acquires a third voice. Up to now it has been Graham's eye and my running commentary. From here on, when Charlotte's observation is sharper than ours, she gets the credit and the line. This one is hers.
