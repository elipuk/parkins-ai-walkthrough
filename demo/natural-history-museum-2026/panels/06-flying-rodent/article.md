# Flying Rodent

*Photograph by Josef Stefan. People's Choice, Wildlife Photographer of the Year. Ciudad Real, Spain; Nikon Z9 with a 600mm f/4.*

An Iberian lynx, on a dirt path in central Spain, in soft golden light, with one paw fully extended in the universal gesture of a cat batting a thing into the air. The thing is a rodent. The rodent is briefly airborne. The lynx's expression is one of total, absorbed concentration — the look of a creature for whom this is, genuinely, the best moment of the afternoon.

The People's Choice award almost picked itself. WPY has plenty of solemn images this year — endangered seals, oiled birds, ice that should be there and isn't — and then here is a six-thousand-pound predator with tufted ears and an aristocratic profile, doing exactly the thing your average tabby does to a feather on a string. All that evolutionary grandeur. All that big-cat lineage. Spent, in this frame, on launching a small mammal three inches off the ground.

(For the record — the lynx is one of conservation's quiet wins. They were down to about a hundred animals at the start of this century, in two tiny populations, and a careful, decades-long reintroduction effort has them back over two thousand. So *Flying Rodent* is, technically, a recovery photograph. It just happens to also be the funniest thing in the room.)

Graham caught the whole exhibition wing in this one. The world is, sometimes, a serious place. And sometimes a cat is just a cat with a snack.
