# Hey Look at Me, I Can Fly

> *"Hey look at me! I can fly!"*
> — Graham

Black and white. The mouth of a stone tunnel or cave, a wash of pale light spilling through it, and bats — half a dozen visible, dozens more implied — pouring out into the evening. One large bat is right in the foreground, body slightly turned, wings *fully* spread, every finger extended in a perfect taut fan. He is, very clearly, the protagonist of this picture. The others are extras.

Graham's caption is correct on three different levels.

**Level one — the obvious one.** The foreground bat is, in fact, *showing off.* You can't photograph that pose without it reading as showing off. He's not flying *through* the frame; he's flying *at* the camera, the way a child holds out a drawing.

**Level two — the anatomical one.** That spread you're looking at? Those aren't bird wings, with feathers and a leading edge. Those are *hands.* The bony fan you can see, from the leading edge of the wing to the trailing tip, is a fully articulated mammalian hand — five digits, the same basic plan you have in yours, the same plan all mammals carry — stretched out and webbed with a sheet of skin. A bat flies by *holding out its fingers very wide.* When the photograph is stripped to black and white, you can see it. You can practically count the knuckles. Your own hand, evolved sideways, given to the air.

**Level three — the lineage one.** Bats are the only mammals that have ever managed proper, flapping, going-where-you-want flight. Pterosaurs did it, birds do it, insects had it cracked before the dinosaurs, but among the four thousand-odd mammal species on this planet, *one* lineage figured out how to launch off a perch and stay up there. The previous panel had a [sloth on a fence post](../15-wrong-turn/), unable to bridge the gap to the next tree because the canopy is gone. This one has the cousin who solved the same problem permanently, tens of millions of years ago, by growing the gap into a wing.

So yes. Hey. Look at me. I *can* fly.

You can hear the small pride in the picture. It is entirely deserved.
